# Notes: memory consolidation during sleep

During sleep the brain replays and reorganizes the day's experiences. The
hippocampus consolidates short-term memories into long-term storage in the
cortex, strengthening important connections and pruning noise.

This is the metaphor behind the "nightly" run: instead of just storing notes,
the system periodically steps back and looks for themes and connections across
everything it has seen — the equivalent of consolidation.

A useful side effect of replay is discovering links between ideas that were
recorded separately. Two notes written weeks apart might turn out to be about
the same underlying problem. Surfacing those connections is where a second
brain earns its keep.
