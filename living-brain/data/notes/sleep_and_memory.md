# Sleep and memory consolidation

During deep sleep the hippocampus replays the day's experiences to the
neocortex. This replay is how short-term memories become long-term ones.

Sharp-wave ripples during slow-wave sleep compress the day's sequences and
play them back at high speed. The brain is effectively rehearsing.

Spindles and slow oscillations coordinate the transfer. Interrupting deep
sleep measurably hurts next-day recall of newly learned material.

The practical takeaway: if you learn something and then sleep, you remember it
far better than if you cram and stay awake. Sleep is not passive downtime.
